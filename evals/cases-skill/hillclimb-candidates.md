Seen by hand in the smoke runs and the transcripts the suite was built from. They are leads, not patches: use one only if the failure summary supports it.

1. Wait scoping contradicts itself. "The commands" says to scope every `wait --pickup` with `--id`, or `--label` with `--worker`. "Messages from the human" describes the listening wait as `--pickup --worker NAME` with no `--id` and no label. Agents without a label copy the second form. One consistent rule for scoping would settle it.
2. Acting on a withdraw reason. When the human withdraws a case the agent has already acted on, and the reason asks for something ("leave it as it is"), agents stop and report but leave their half-done change in place. The skill says only "stop the work it asked about" and "say what you had done"; it says nothing about the withdraw's reason being an instruction to follow.
3. Cost of a simple decision. With the skill loaded, a single simple decision cost about twice as much as without it ($0.23 against $0.12 in one smoke pair), mostly the skill's own context plus an extra call to read the wait output. SKILL.md is loaded in full every session; moving rarely needed text to the reference, or tightening it, cuts every session's cost.
4. The open is not chained with its wait. In real sessions after #78, 1 of 6 sessions chained every open with its wait (4 of 28 before). Agents open in one call and start the wait in the next, which is the extra call the workflow section exists to remove. (Offline audit of real sessions, 2026-09-29.)
5. Detached waits. Real sessions start `cases wait` with a shell `&`, `disown`, or output sent to /dev/null, instead of the tool's background: 9 such calls in 6 sessions before #78, 2 calls in 2 of 6 sessions after. A detached pickup wait takes the answer and nobody reads it. SKILL.md says to run the wait in the background but never says not to detach it. (Audit, 2026-09-29.)
6. `cases status` in a call of its own. 25 status-only calls in real sessions (21 before #78, 4 after); only 3 of 6 sessions after #78 ran status beside their first open, as SKILL.md asks. (Audit, 2026-09-29.)
7. Extra calls per decision did not fall measurably with #78: 3.04 before, 2.86 after, and the cost of the cases-only calls per decision went from $0.309 to $0.320. The rewrite did not remove the extra round trips it set out to cut. (Audit, 2026-09-29; 6 sessions after #78, so treat as a lead.)
